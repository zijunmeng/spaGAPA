# spaGAPA: Abstract, Introduction, and Discussion

**Companion to:** *spaGAPA: A Statistical Framework for Spatial Alternative Polyadenylation Analysis with Calibrated Uncertainty Quantification* (Methods + Results in `MANUSCRIPT_DRAFT.md`).

**Target journal:** Briefings in Bioinformatics (BIB)

## Abstract

**Background.** Spatial transcriptomics now makes it possible to measure alternative polyadenylation (APA) — the choice among competing 3′-end sites that shapes 3′-UTR length and, in turn, miRNA- and RBP-mediated regulation — directly in tissue context. However, existing spatial APA tools deliver only point estimates of polyadenylation-site (PAS) usage and provide no mechanism to decide how much to trust each estimate, while their O(n²) neighbor-graph implementations fail to scale to the new subcellular-resolution platforms. The field therefore lacks a downstream inference framework that is simultaneously probabilistic, calibrated, and scalable.

**Methods.** We present spaGAPA, a downstream statistical inference framework that operates on a PAS × spot usage matrix produced by any external PAS caller (here, scAPAtrap). spaGAPA casts spatial APA imputation as sparse Gaussian-process (GP) regression with O(nm²) cost, augments the GP posterior with residual-based heteroscedastic noise estimation, and wraps the result in a split-conformal prediction layer that delivers a distribution-free *marginal* coverage guarantee under exchangeability. We emphasize that spaGAPA is not a PAS caller; its contribution is the calibrated probabilistic treatment of the sparse, noisy, spatially structured APA matrix that PAS calling produces.

**Results.** Across 11 Visium samples (7 GSE datasets, 4 tissues, 2 species, 523,174 held-out points), split-conformal coverage was within 0.2% of nominal at the 80/90/95% targets and remained within ±5 percentage points across spatial regions and uncertainty strata. In head-to-head benchmarking, although a per-gene mean achieved lower global RMSE, spaGAPA better recovered spatial variation (spatial-fidelity 0.42 vs 0.00) and was 5.7–7.2× faster than stAPAminer and spvAPA. The conformal half-width gave an actionable uncertainty score: uncertainty-ranked retention outperformed random retention by 23.2% RMSE at 80% retention (p = 0.004). spaGAPA completed 42,000- and 100,000-spot analyses under predefined resource limits where comparator implementations did not, and ran end-to-end on a subcellular Stereo-seq sample (21,455 PAS × 20.7 M DNBs). At the donor level (n = 3 per group), no gene reached FDR significance in differential APA; spot-level results are reported as exploratory.

**Conclusion.** spaGAPA is, to our knowledge, the first spatial APA framework to combine calibrated, distribution-free uncertainty quantification with scalability to subcellular-resolution platforms, and is positioned as a downstream inference layer compatible with existing PAS callers.

## Introduction

Alternative polyadenylation (APA) is the co-transcriptional and post-transcriptional choice among multiple polyadenylation sites (PAS) within a gene, generating mRNA isoforms that differ in the length and regulatory content of their 3′-untranslated regions (3′-UTRs). Because 3′-UTRs harbor the binding sites for the majority of miRNAs and RNA-binding proteins (RBPs), the relative use of proximal versus distal PAS — and the resulting shortening or lengthening of the 3′-UTR — directly controls transcript stability, localization, and translational efficiency. APA is pervasive: more than half of human multi-exon genes produce alternative 3′-ends, and systematic perturbation of 3′-end-processing machinery reshapes cell-state-specific transcriptomes in development, immune activation, neurodegeneration, and cancer. Quantifying where, in a tissue, a gene switches its PAS usage is therefore a direct readout of post-transcriptional regulatory heterogeneity.

Spatial transcriptomics has transformed how we measure this heterogeneity. 10x Visium captures genome-wide gene expression on a regular grid of spots (≈55 µm diameter, ≈100 µm spacing), and emerging subcellular-resolution platforms — Stereo-seq, Slide-seqV2, and related DNB-array chemistries — push measurement to near-single-molecule resolution across whole-tissue sections. Several recent studies have extended APA quantification into this spatial regime: by re-running single-cell APA pipelines (scAPAtrap, Sierra, SCAPE, MAAPER) on position-resolved reads, it is now possible to assemble a PAS × spot matrix of poly(A)-end usage over a tissue section. Spatial APA measurements are, however, qualitatively harder than their single-cell counterparts for two reasons. First, they are *extremely sparse*: because each spot captures a small fraction of the poly(A) reads of any given gene, the gene-level distal-usage ratio is frequently estimated from one to five reads and is undefined (missing) at the majority of gene–spot entries — typically ≈90% missingness per dataset. Second, they are *spatially structured*: neighboring spots share both transcriptional state and local 3′-end-processing context, so the APA matrix carries a spatial autocorrelation that single-cell APA pipelines do not exploit. These two properties motivate a dedicated statistical treatment rather than the direct transplantation of single-cell APA estimators.

A small but growing set of tools has begun to address spatial APA directly. stAPAminer applies k-nearest-neighbor (KNN) imputation in gene-expression space to denoise a PAS × spot matrix, providing smoothed APA maps and downstream spatially variable APA (SVAPA) calls. spvAPA replaces KNN with weighted nearest-neighbor (WNN) integration of expression and APA modalities and adds supervised sparse partial-least-squares discriminant analysis (sPLS-DA) for domain classification. metaAPA integrates PAS calls across multiple studies but is not, by design, a spatial inference tool. An independent benchmark survey (Li et al., 2024) has evaluated *PAS callers* — the upstream step that produces the peaks feeding each of these frameworks — but has not evaluated the downstream spatial-inference problem on which stAPAminer, spvAPA, and the present work actually differ. The present manuscript addresses that downstream-inference problem.

Two structural gaps remain in the spatial APA toolset. The first is the *uncertainty gap*. None of the existing spatial APA tools reports any quantification of how much to trust each per-gene per-spot usage estimate, yet on a matrix that is ≈90% missing this question is the central one. Many estimates rest on a handful of reads; a PAS-usage gradient that looks striking on a smoothed map may be supported by a noise floor indistinguishable from sampling error. Without calibrated intervals, downstream differential APA, SVAPA, and domain calls cannot be triaged by reliability. Heuristic smoothing does not solve this: a KNN- or WNN-imputed value carries no principled posterior variance, and bootstrapping a per-gene variance is itself a noisy estimate on a handful of reads. What is required is a framework whose predicted intervals carry a finite-sample, distribution-free coverage guarantee — together with an explicit, audited statement of what that guarantee does and does not cover.

The second gap is the *scalability gap*. KNN- and WNN-based spatial APA methods are inherently O(n²) in the number of spots n, because each spot's imputed value is a weighted aggregate over its neighbors and the neighbor graph itself must be built and queried over all pairs. This is tractable on a Visium section (n ≈ 1.5–6 k) but is the binding bottleneck at higher resolution. Stereo-seq sections routinely comprise tens of millions of DNBs; even mid-resolution Visium-HD and binned Stereo-seq matrices reach tens of thousands of spots. At these scales the O(n²) neighbor evaluation becomes the dominant cost, and tools that work gracefully on Visium either time out or exhaust memory. As high-resolution spatial platforms become the default rather than the exception, spatial APA analysis needs an inference framework whose per-spot cost is sub-quadratic in n.

To address both gaps simultaneously, we introduce spaGAPA, a downstream statistical inference framework for spatial APA. spaGAPA is built around three components. First, it casts per-gene spatial APA imputation as a sparse Gaussian-process (GP) regression over the two-dimensional tissue coordinates, using an inducing-point approximation that reduces per-gene cost to O(nm²) with m ≪ n inducing points and a precomputed cross-covariance matrix K_nm shared across all genes. The GP posterior provides a principled mean (the imputed APA usage) and a posterior standard deviation (a kernel-driven smoothing uncertainty) for every gene–spot entry. Second, it augments the GP posterior with a residual-based heteroscedastic noise model that captures gene- and spot-specific observation noise, which on sparse APA data is strongly non-homogeneous. Third, it wraps the heteroscedastic GP in a split-conformal prediction layer that delivers a distribution-free *marginal* coverage guarantee under exchangeability, with the finite-sample quantile correction needed for the guarantee to hold in finite samples.

We are explicit about two boundaries of these contributions. First, the conformal guarantee is *marginal*, not conditional: averaging over all test entries, empirical coverage is at least 1 − α in expectation, but the coverage of any particular subgroup (a single tissue region, a single uncertainty quintile, a single expression stratum) is not individually guaranteed and must be checked empirically — which we do across four subgroup axes (Results R5). Where subgroup coverage is found to break down — specifically, in the extreme expression tails — we report it as a limitation rather than smoothing over it. Second, the raw GP posterior standard deviation is a useful global risk-stratification signal but a modest within-gene ranking signal: pooled uncertainty/error correlation is r ≈ 0.55, whereas the median per-gene correlation is r ≈ 0.14. The conformal layer provides the marginal coverage guarantee regardless; the heteroscedastic layer provides the global risk stratification; together they support an actionable risk-coverage analysis (Results R6) but do not deliver conditional coverage guarantees.

We position spaGAPA deliberately as a *downstream inference framework*, not as a PAS caller. Its required input is a PAS × spot usage matrix together with per-spot coordinates, which we obtain from scAPAtrap (the top-ranked PAS caller in the Li et al. 2024 benchmark) but which could equally be produced by Sierra, SCAPE, or MAAPER. By holding the PAS-calling step fixed across all method comparisons, we remove PAS-caller differences as a confounder and isolate the contribution of the downstream spatial-inference component. The framework additionally provides unsupervised spatial-domain identification (M7), donor-level differential APA with explicit pseudoreplication safeguards (M11), spatially-variable APA scoring, APA-specific batch correction, and an end-to-end Stereo-seq processing module (M10). These modules are described in the Methods and reported in the Results, but they are not the primary contribution; the primary contribution is the calibrated, scalable spatial-APA inference layer.

The remainder of the manuscript is organized as follows. Results R1 introduces the framework. R2–R3 report a transparent head-to-head benchmark against stAPAminer, spvAPA, a generic spatial-KNN baseline, and a per-gene mean baseline, in which we report the regime in which the per-gene mean dominates entry-wise RMSE and the regime in which the named competitors are the appropriate comparison. R4–R6 validate the conformal coverage guarantee across 11 datasets, audit subgroup coverage on four axes, and characterize the heteroscedastic risk-stratification signal and its limits. R7 reports scalability on synthetic matrices up to 100,000 spots. R8 demonstrates end-to-end APA mapping on subcellular Stereo-seq data. R9 reports donor-level and spot-level differential APA, including the honest outcome that no gene reaches FDR significance at the donor level (n = 3). We close with a discussion of methodological contributions, limitations, related work, and future directions.

## Discussion

We have presented spaGAPA, a downstream statistical inference framework for spatial alternative polyadenylation that combines sparse Gaussian-process (GP) regression, heteroscedastic noise estimation, and split-conformal prediction. Across 11 Visium samples spanning four tissues and two species, split-conformal coverage tracked the 80/90/95% nominal targets to within 0.2 percentage points and remained within ±5 percentage points across spatial regions and uncertainty strata. In head-to-head benchmarking, spaGAPA better recovered spatial variation than a per-gene mean baseline (spatial-fidelity 0.42 vs 0.00) while being 5.7–7.2× faster than stAPAminer and spvAPA; although the per-gene mean achieved lower global RMSE on the bimodal gene-level distal-usage index, spaGAPA additionally provided calibrated uncertainty that none of the baselines offers. The framework completed 42,000- and 100,000-spot analyses under predefined resource limits where comparator implementations did not, and ran end-to-end on a subcellular Stereo-seq section (21,455 PAS × 20.7 M DNBs). The conformal uncertainty score was actionable: uncertainty-ranked retention of gene–spot predictions outperformed random retention by 23.2% RMSE at 80% retention (p = 0.004).

**Conformal uncertainty quantification.** The principal methodological contribution is the introduction of distribution-free marginal uncertainty quantification to spatial APA. Split-conformal prediction, by construction, guarantees marginal coverage under exchangeability of the calibration and test entries, and the finite-sample-corrected quantile we use (⌈(n_cal + 1)(1 − α)⌉ / n_cal) makes the guarantee valid in finite samples regardless of the GP posterior's calibration. The empirical marginal coverage we observe — within 0.2 percentage points across all 11 samples and three target levels — is consistent with this guarantee. We stress, and have audited empirically, that the guarantee is *marginal*: conditional coverage on a particular subgroup is not guaranteed and must be verified. Our subgroup audit shows that the guarantee extends empirically across spatial regions (24/24 bins within ±0.05 of nominal) and uncertainty quintiles (20/20 within ±0.05), and an adversarial spatial-block split (3 train blocks, 2 test blocks) returned coverage statistically indistinguishable from the random split, arguing against an exchangeability violation by spatial autocorrelation. The one axis on which calibration broke down was expression stratum: extreme-expression genes undercovered to ≈0.83, with the worst single bin reaching 0.745. Method D's residual-based empirical-Bayes noise partially corrects this (high-expression coverage 0.833) but does not eliminate it. This is the most important limitation of the conformal layer, and we discuss it below.

**Sparse GP and heteroscedastic noise.** The sparse inducing-point approximation reduces per-gene cost from the full GP's O(n³) to O(nm²), with m ≈ 150–200 inducing points for Visium and the cross-covariance K_nm precomputed once across all genes. This is what enables the 42,000- and 100,000-spot completions under the predefined 1200-second wall cap, where the O(n²) neighbor-graph implementations of stAPAminer and spvAPA either timed out or failed with a memory error. We report these outcomes factually as non-completion under the predefined resource limits, and do not claim that the comparators are intrinsically unable to scale: with more generous memory and time budgets their asymptotic constants differ and they may complete at higher scales. The heteroscedastic noise layer (Method D) further turns the kernel-driven GP posterior standard deviation into an informative global risk-stratification signal, with pooled uncertainty/error correlation r ≈ 0.55. We are explicit that within-gene ranking is more modest (median per-gene r ≈ 0.14; per-gene centered r ≈ 0.11), because the pooled correlation is partly driven by cross-gene heterogeneity in average uncertainty and error. Nonetheless, the global stratification is actionable: the risk-coverage curve shows that ranking gene–spot predictions by Method D's conformal half-width and retaining the lowest-uncertainty 80% reduces RMSE by 23.2% relative to random retention at the same fraction (p = 0.004), with the improvement growing monotonically as the retention fraction decreases (65.8% at 20%). The uncertainty score is therefore useful for downstream triage in the global-risk-stratification sense.

**Transparent benchmarking.** A central methodological choice was to include a per-gene mean baseline explicitly and to report the regime in which it dominates entry-wise RMSE. The gene-level distal-usage index is strongly bimodal — most genes are nearly all-proximal or nearly all-distal — so a per-gene constant that predicts the dominant mode minimizes entry-wise RMSE while erasing all spatial variation. A report that omitted this baseline would overstate spaGAPA's accuracy claim; a report that framed spaGAPA as "most accurate" would be false. We instead frame the contribution as best spatial fidelity (0.42 vs the mean's 0.00), best calibrated uncertainty (which the mean cannot provide), and best scalability (5.7–7.2× faster than the named competitors, and the only method to complete at 42 k and 100 k spots under the predefined resource limits). The named competitors — stAPAminer and spvAPA — are the appropriate comparison for spatial fidelity, uncertainty, and runtime; the per-gene mean is the appropriate comparison for entry-wise RMSE; and we report both comparisons side by side.

**Limitations.** We are explicit about five. First, the raw GP uncertainty is a cross-gene-dominant signal: pooled uncertainty/error correlation is r ≈ 0.55, but the median per-gene correlation is r ≈ 0.14, and 17–26% of genes exceed r = 0.30 in within-gene uncertainty/error correlation. spaGAPA therefore supports reliable global risk stratification and modest within-gene discrimination, but a downstream analysis that requires per-gene error ranking — for example, ranking spots within a single highly variable gene — should treat the uncertainty score as a weak signal. Second, the conformal guarantee is marginal, and we observe expression-dependent undercoverage (≈0.83 in the highest-expression stratum) that Method D's residual-based noise only partially corrects; users requiring conditional coverage on expression strata should not rely on the marginal guarantee alone. Third, differential APA at the spot level is a pseudoreplication if spots are treated as independent replicates; the confirmatory analysis is at the donor level, and at n = 3 per group no gene reached FDR significance in GSE220442 (696 testable genes). Spot-level differential APA results are reported as exploratory only, with a direction-agreement filter nominating 29 hypothesis-generating candidates; we emphasize that the absence of FDR significance at n = 3 is a statistical-boundary statement and not a negative biological result. Fourth, Stereo-seq APA analysis is constrained by a field-wide data-availability bottleneck: of the GEO-deposited Stereo-seq datasets we surveyed, only one had both the raw FASTQ and the tissue mask required for end-to-end SAW + scAPAtrap processing. The descriptive APA mapping we report (mouse AD brain, 21,455 PAS × 20.7 M DNBs) is therefore a feasibility demonstration rather than a comparative disease analysis, and the available AD-versus-WT Stereo-seq replication (n = 1 per condition) permits only effect-size reporting. Fifth, spaGAPA is unsupervised and does not currently offer supervised domain classification or discriminant analysis; spvAPA's sPLS-DA module is a complementary capability we do not duplicate.

**Relation to related work.** spaGAPA is complementary to stAPAminer and spvAPA rather than a strict replacement. stAPAminer provides smoothed APA maps and SVAPA calls via expression-space KNN; spvAPA provides multimodal WNN integration and supervised sPLS-DA classification. Neither provides calibrated uncertainty, and neither scales to the high-resolution regime under the resource limits we tested. spaGAPA trades supervised classification for conformal uncertainty and sub-quadratic scalability, and operates on a PAS × spot matrix produced by the same scAPAtrap pipeline that an independent benchmark (Li et al., 2024) identified as the top PAS caller for differential-APA detection — making the three frameworks interoperable at the input level. More broadly, calibrated uncertainty quantification is well developed for the gene-expression modality (scVI, BayesSpace, totalVI, and related deep-generative and Bayesian spatial models), but the spatial APA problem differs structurally: the APA matrix is bounded in [0, 1], compositional (proximal + distal = 1 within a gene), and ~90% missing, whereas expression-model UQ targets unbounded count matrices. The conformal layer we introduce is modality-agnostic in principle, but the heteroscedastic noise estimation, the binning strategy for subcellular platforms, and the bimodal-index benchmark baseline are APA-specific choices that distinguish this framework from expression-modality UQ methods.

**Future directions.** Three extensions are natural. First, the marginal conformal guarantee can be tightened toward *conditional* coverage by Mondrian or expression-stratified conformal calibration, in which separate quantiles are estimated for each expression stratum; this would directly address the expression-dependent undercoverage we observe (≈0.83) and is the most direct remedy for the principal conditional-coverage limitation. Second, the current spatial GP operates on the APA modality alone; a multimodal GP that integrates the gene-expression modality probabilistically — analogous to spvAPA's WNN integration but with calibrated uncertainty rather than a point-estimate fusion — could improve within-gene uncertainty ranking (currently r ≈ 0.14) without abandoning the marginal coverage guarantee. Third, the donor-level differential APA analysis is statistically underpowered at n = 3; confirmatory testing on larger disease cohorts is required before any of the 29 hypothesis-generating candidates can be reported as confirmed differential APA. We view these as the most consequential next steps for moving the framework from calibrated spatial mapping toward calibrated spatial discovery.

**Conclusion.** spaGAPA introduces calibrated, distribution-free uncertainty quantification and sub-quadratic scalability to spatial alternative polyadenylation analysis, and demonstrates that the resulting framework runs end-to-end from 10x Visium to subcellular-resolution Stereo-seq. We position it as a downstream inference layer — not a PAS caller — compatible with existing PAS-calling pipelines, and report its coverage guarantee, spatial fidelity, runtime, and limitations with the explicit boundaries required of a methods contribution.
